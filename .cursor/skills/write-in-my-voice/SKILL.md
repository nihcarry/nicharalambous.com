# Write in My Voice

When the user asks you to help write an article (or other content) in their voice, use the documents in **nicharalambous-voice-training/** as the source of truth for style, tone, and patterns. Your output should match that voice as closely as possible.

## Voice training sources

All in `nicharalambous-voice-training/`:

- **DO_FAIL_LEARN_REPEAT_Nic_Book.md** – Book; reflective, honest, personal stories, failure/learning.
- **Business_Builders_Toolkit_Nic_Book.doc.md** – Book; practical, direct, frameworks, examples.
- **Reclaiming-Focus-in-a-World-That-Profits-From-Your-Distraction_Keynote.md** – Keynote; focus, tech, health, sacrifice fallacy.
- **Curiosity_Catalyst_keynote_transcript.md** – Keynote; curiosity, innovation vs wackovation, stagnation.
- **how_to_build_breakthrough_Product_teams_Keynote.md** – Keynote; entrepreneurial teams, permission to fail, AI/culture.

## Workflow when writing

1. **Calibrate** – Read or skim relevant sections from the voice-training docs for the topic (e.g. failure/learning from the book, focus from the keynote). For quick checks use [voice-traits.md](voice-traits.md).
2. **Draft** – Write in first person, direct and honest. Use the traits below; avoid corporate, generic, or influencer fluff.
3. **Revise** – Compare the draft to a short sample from the training docs. Tighten sentences, add a concrete example or rhetorical question if missing, and cut anything that doesn’t sound like the reference.

## Voice traits (apply these)

- **Direct and honest** – Say what you mean. No hedging (“perhaps we might consider”). Call out BS when relevant (“I call that bullshit”, “That’s absolute bullshit” where it fits).
- **First person, personal** – Own experience, vulnerability, and specific stories (kidney stones, matric exams, Zune vs iPod, Reed Hoffman vs Jason from Basecamp).
- **Short punchy + longer reflective** – Mix short sentences with longer ones but make sure the flow and pace are not too abrasive. e.g. “Building things is hard. Building a business is extremely hard. It’s meant to be hard.”
- **Concrete over abstract** – One clear example or story beats three abstract principles. Names, products, quotes (Edison, Paul Graham, Peter Thiel) when they fit.
- **Reframe conventional wisdom** – Challenge clichés: “Innovation is an outcome… It’s not an action you can take.” “A lack of AI is not killing your business. A culture of caution is.”
- **Address the reader as “you”** – Inclusive, conversational. “If you’re reading this…”, “You don’t have to trust what I say either.”
- **Memorable phrases and frameworks** – “Do. Fail. Learn. Repeat.”, “sacrifice fallacy”, “wackovation”, “innovation theater”, “just avoid dying”. Create or reuse 1–2 in each piece when it fits.
- **Self-deprecating but confident** – “This young idiot believed…”, “I’m that irritating child”, “I’ve probably failed more than anyone” — then land the point with conviction.
- **Occasional sharp language** – “bullshit”, “fucking hard work”, “fire the assholes” only when it matches the tone of the source material; don’t force it.
- **No fake positivity** – Acknowledge difficulty, failure, and cost. “Starting a business will not make you happy.” “This shit is hard and it’s meant to be.”
- **Introduce some softness** – While Nic's style is sharp, harsh and brutal, the Internet loves positivity, abundance and a softer approach. So the overall tone should slant more towards this than abrasive, aggressive, harsh or overly brutal. 

## What to avoid

- Corporate jargon, buzzwords, or vague “thought leadership” without a clear point.
- Generic influencer tone (hustle culture, vague inspiration, “you’ve got this”).
- Overly formal or academic phrasing.
- Advice that sounds like everyone else; if it could be from any blog, rewrite it in this voice.

## Optional: deeper calibration

For a long or high-stakes piece, read 2–3 full sections from the most relevant voice-training file (e.g. a chapter from DO_FAIL_LEARN_REPEAT or a full keynote transcript) before drafting, then compare your draft to that passage and adjust.

## Output

Deliver the article (or section) in markdown. If the user gave a topic or outline, follow it but in this voice. Offer one optional alternative opening or closing line where it might help.
