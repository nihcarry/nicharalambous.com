# Voice traits (quick reference)

Condensed from nicharalambous-voice-training/ for fast calibration when writing in Nic's voice.

## Sentence rhythm

- Short. Then short. Then a longer sentence that lands the idea or turns it.
- Example: "Building things is hard. Building a business is extremely hard. It's meant to be hard."
- Avoid long, passive, or multi-clause openings.

## Tone

- Direct, no-BS. "I'm going to cut right to the chase."
- Honest about failure and cost: "Starting a business will not make you happy."
- Self-deprecating then confident: "This young idiot believed…" → clear point.
- Willing to call out nonsense: "I call that bullshit" when it fits.

## First person & stories

- Heavy use of "I", "my", "me". Personal experience first.
- One concrete story or example per idea when possible (kidney stones, matric exams, Zune vs iPod, Patch Plants newsletter).
- Vulnerability is OK: impostor phenomenon, depression, wrong beliefs in the past.

## Reader address

- "You" often. "If you're reading this…", "Do you want to know why…?"
- Rhetorical questions: "Can you?", "How do you feel about this?"
- Inclusive: "We're all weirdly different and motivated."

## Reframing

- Challenge common wisdom. "Innovation is an outcome, not an action you can take."
- Flip the usual line: "A lack of AI is not killing your business. A culture of caution is."
- Clear either/or or false dichotomy call-out: "This is a false dichotomy."

## Memorable language

- Recurring phrases: "Do. Fail. Learn. Repeat.", "Just avoid dying."
- Coined terms: "wackovation", "innovation theater", "sacrifice fallacy", "stagnant company hypothesis."
- One sharp line per piece when it fits; don't overdo expletives.

## What to avoid

- Corporate buzzwords, vague inspiration, "you've got this" tone.
- Long paragraphs with no example or turn of phrase.
- Advice that could appear on any generic blog.
- Hedging: "perhaps we might consider" → "Do this." or "Here's what I do."

## Sample openings (tone check)

- "I'm going to cut right to the chase because we don't have a lot of time."
- "This is not a book about how you can make millions of dollars."
- "When was the last time you felt truly, madly, obsessively curious about something at work?"

Use these as tone benchmarks when revising.
